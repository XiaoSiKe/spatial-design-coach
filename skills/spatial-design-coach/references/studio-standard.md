# Studio Assignment Standard

Read this reference when mapping a brief or rubric, checking a stage gate, deciding whether an assignment is complete, or calibrating architecture, urban-planning, landscape-architecture, or joint-studio depth.

## Apply the right precedence

Use this order:

1. the supplied project brief, rubric, tutor clarification, and submission instructions;
2. current safety, legal, accessibility, environmental, structural, and academic-integrity requirements that actually apply;
3. the common studio gates below to fill gaps and expose contradictions.

Do not use a generic gate to erase a course-specific requirement. Do not let a course request authorize fabricated evidence, false compliance, impersonated authorship, or unsafe professional certainty.

Map every retained requirement to one responsible Artifact and one observable pass condition in `studio/PROJECT.md`. “Satisfied” without evidence is not a result.

## Use eight non-compensating gates

1. **Brief compliance:** program, boundary, scale, format, deadline and required deliverables are accounted for.
2. **Problem validity:** the site, affected people or system, conflict and realistic project agency are specific; the proposition can be challenged.
3. **Decision-changing evidence:** retained analysis, precedent, theory and data support, weaken or change a design move.
4. **Real alternatives:** 2–3 families differ in mechanism, spatial organization or value trade-off while holding brief compliance constant.
5. **Spatial resolution:** plan, section, scale, circulation, sequence, edge, ground and time make the proposition visible.
6. **Disciplinary development:** technical depth matches the course level; uncertainty and required professional verification are explicit.
7. **Result consistency:** drawings, models, boards, text, quantities and speech use one project version and prove complementary claims.
8. **Defensible authorship:** the student can explain the proposition, evidence, mechanism, trade-off, limitation and rejected alternative in their own words.

A fatal failure at one gate is not repaired by polish elsewhere. Do not average the gates into one score.

## Select disciplinary checks

### Architecture

Check program and adjacency, bodily scale, circulation and egress requiring verification, public/private thresholds, structure, envelope, climate, services, material, construction and adaptability. Demand plan–section agreement before accepting atmosphere or form.

### Urban planning and design

Check land and density, mobility and network hierarchy, public services, population and affected groups, policy and governance, implementation, phasing, economics and distributive effect. Do not let indicators remain colored zones without lived spatial consequences.

### Landscape architecture

Check terrain, water, soil, planting, habitat, succession, season, disturbance, public use, establishment and maintenance. Do not accept green color, species lists or rendering as ecological performance.

### Joint studio

Keep one spatial index, datum, project version, legend and state convention. Trace human movement and relevant water, habitat, energy, material or service flows across scale. At each disciplinary seam, record the decision owner and downstream Artifacts.

## Calibrate to course depth

- Early studio: require clear relationships, scale, sequence, evidence and representation; label technical assumptions.
- Intermediate studio: require alternative comparison, cross-scale consistency, environmental and structural logic, and more resolved plans and sections.
- Advanced or thesis studio: require independent research framing, implementation and governance, robust technical verification, acknowledged counterevidence and a coherent final result set.

Do not lower truthfulness, access, safety or authorship standards for an early student. Adjust depth and representation, not rigor.

## Declare completion honestly

Call an assignment complete only when:

- every confirmed required deliverable has a final file and completed QA;
- all final Artifacts use the same selected project state;
- the central proposition is visible in the strongest plan and section or equivalent disciplinary evidence;
- assumptions and missing professional checks are disclosed;
- the oral presentation has been timed and rehearsed;
- the student has confirmed pivotal design choices.

If time ends first, state what is complete, provisional, missing and unsafe to claim. A coherent limited submission is stronger than a falsely complete one.
