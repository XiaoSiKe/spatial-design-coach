# Contemporary Design Challenges

Read this reference only when a live project decision involves one or more of the following challenges. Use it to sharpen a spatial decision, not to add fashionable labels or a sustainability checklist.

For any selected challenge, complete five links:

```text
decision → spatial mechanism → evidence → trade-off → verification
```

## Existing stock, sufficiency, and circularity

- **Decision:** What can remain, adapt, be shared, or be removed before adding new construction?
- **Spatial mechanism:** Retention, reversible intervention, adaptable frame, disassembly, reuse flow, shared capacity.
- **Evidence:** Existing condition, cultural value, capacity, material inventory, lifecycle boundary, realistic reuse chain.
- **Trade-off:** Retention may constrain program or performance; replacement spends carbon, memory, money, and time.
- **Verification:** Comparative retain/adapt/replace scenario with quantities and assumptions appropriate to the studio level.

## Whole-life carbon and resource restraint

- **Decision:** Which spatial or program choice reduces demand rather than only improving equipment efficiency?
- **Spatial mechanism:** Compactness, passive comfort, shared program, material economy, long life, flexible use.
- **Evidence:** Climate, occupancy, area, material, energy and lifecycle assumptions; current-source calculation when consequential.
- **Trade-off:** Lower area or material demand can conflict with comfort, access, identity, revenue, or future flexibility.
- **Verification:** Side-by-side demand, area, material or lifecycle scenario; label unverified performance.

## Climate adaptation and nature-based systems

- **Decision:** Which hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and recovery capacity should the project change?
- **Spatial mechanism:** Shade, ventilation, water retention, safe route, refuge, habitat continuity, redundancy, adaptive edge.
- **Evidence:** Current climate/hazard source, topography, hydrology, ecology, affected groups, maintenance and governance.
- **Trade-off:** Protection can transfer risk, consume land, exclude users, simplify ecology, or fail without stewardship.
- **Verification:** Event or seasonal scenario across the project boundary; specialist analysis when required.

## Spatial justice, access, and displacement

- **Decision:** Who gains access, benefit, voice, safety, visibility, or tenure—and who bears cost or displacement risk?
- **Spatial mechanism:** Distribution, connection, threshold, universal access, mixed use, protected use, anti-displacement delivery.
- **Evidence:** Affected groups, daily routes, service gaps, tenure/governance conditions, barriers, direct participation where possible.
- **Trade-off:** A universally framed public benefit can erase conflicting needs or accelerate exclusion.
- **Verification:** Distributional map or user journey plus a procedural account of who influenced the decision.

## Participation, care, and maintenance

- **Decision:** Which choices require shared authorship, and who can operate, repair, finance, or adapt the result?
- **Spatial mechanism:** Co-design point, stewardship space, maintenance access, visible infrastructure, phased ownership.
- **Evidence:** Stakeholder roles, capacity, labor, funding, seasonal tasks, conflict and decision authority.
- **Trade-off:** Participation can be extractive or symbolic; low-maintenance claims can hide unpaid care.
- **Verification:** Decision-rights and maintenance map connected to plans, sections, phasing, and budget assumptions.

## Phasing, uncertainty, and long-term change

- **Decision:** What must be fixed now, what can remain open, and what signal should trigger the next phase?
- **Spatial mechanism:** Increment, reserve, adaptable frame, temporary use, succession, reversible threshold, monitoring point.
- **Evidence:** Dependency, funding, growth, disturbance, demand range, ecological time, institutional capacity.
- **Trade-off:** Flexibility can become vagueness; fixed optimization can become brittle or prematurely expensive.
- **Verification:** At least two time states plus trigger, responsible actor, failure condition, and recoverable path.

## Combine without checklist inflation

Select only challenges that can change a live decision. When several apply, find a shared spatial mechanism or expose the conflict between them. Do not claim that a project is sustainable, just, resilient, circular, or participatory because the vocabulary appears; point to the artifact and verification that support the claim.
